\title{Non-Local Boundary Address Relabeling and the Modular State Translocator in Static Holographic Boundary Theory}
\author{SHBT Collaboration}
\noaffiliation
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Static Holographic Boundary Theory (SHBT) treats a bulk destination not as an independent spacetime point but as a rendered image of boundary information.
We present the production-grade \emph{Modular State Translocator} for the canonical anomaly-free branch $(26,8,312)$.
The translocator first de-renders a visible boundary character block through an isometric Stinespring dilation, deposits the removed information into a dark ledger partitioned by the exact rational capacities $10/33$ and $23/33$, and re-renders the state at a future-authorized boundary address using a phase-locked excitation and a Heegaard--Floer boundary relabeling.
We prove that the Stinespring map preserves the information density of the completed sector, derive the reconstruction operator $R^{\text{rerender}}$, and show that passive stress-energy is conserved once the active metric slice is nullified.
A hardware realization is described using an InP/InGaAs single heterojunction bipolar transistor (SHBT) with a micro-airbridge structure ($f_{\max}=72$~GHz) and a 2D topological-insulator edge-state waveguide.
The reference Rust/Python implementation executes at 512-bit precision and reports a Stinespring ratio of $\ReconStinespringRatio$, a unitarity residual below $\ReconUnitarityTarget$, an eigenvector-rigidity detuning below $\ReconDetuningTarget$, and a per-GET thermodynamic cost of $\ReconCGetJ$~J/bit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and SHBT context}
\label{sec:intro}

Static Holographic Boundary Theory (SHBT) posits that spacetime geometry and matter are rendered images of boundary information, encoded in a finite-dimensional register of character excitations.
The present work extends the engineering benchmarks reported for the $(26,8,312)$ branch in \texttt{shbt-precision}\cite{shbtprecision} and the Alcubierre-type longitudinal shift metrics of \texttt{shbt-warp}\cite{shbtwarp}, where a $10\,\mathrm{m}$ warp bubble required an operational power of $142.08\,\mathrm{MW}$ while satisfying the weak and null energy conditions on the interior plateau.

In SHBT, a bulk event is not a primitive object; it is the emergent image of a pattern of boundary character excitations.
The ``closure chain'' that protects the theory from unphysical geometries is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:closure-chain}
\text{modular invariance}
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\Delta_{\mathrm{fr}}=0
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
E_{\mu\nu}=0,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{\mathrm{fr}}$ is the scalar framing defect of the boundary register and $E_{\mu\nu}$ is the holographic stress-energy residual.
When the chain holds, the boundary is anomaly-free and the rendered geometry is geometrically and energetically consistent.

This paper concerns the next logical operation: not merely rendering a bulk slice, but \emph{de-rendering} it---i.e.~nullifying the active metric degrees of freedom while preserving the passive stress-energy---and subsequently \emph{re-rendering} a destination state at a new boundary address.
We model this as a Stinespring dilation on the boundary Hilbert space, followed by a phase-locked reconstruction operator, and we describe the hardware platform required to execute the protocol at microwave-clock rates.

\section{First-principles derivations}
\label{sec:derivations}

\subsection{The Stinespring dilation as artificial de-rendering}

Consider a visible boundary register $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{vis}}$ of dimension $K=312$ and a dark ledger space $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{dark}}$ that absorbs the information removed from the active metric.
For implementation, the visible register is tiled into $16$ character blocks, each carrying an $8$-component dark ledger; the entire coupled state vector is the stack-allocated array
\begin{equation}
\Psi = \bigl[\psi_{c,i}\bigr]_{c=0}^{15}\!{}_{i=0}^{7},
\qquad
\psi_{c,i}\in\mathbb{C},
\end{equation}
with no heap allocation, preserving the integrity of high-frequency braiding loops.

The dark ledger is split into two capacity sectors,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dark-split}
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{dark}}
=
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{dark}}^{\mathrm{res}}
\oplus
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{dark}}^{\mathrm{comp}},
\end{equation}
with fixed capacities
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:capacities}
c_{\mathrm{dark}}^{\mathrm{res}}=\frac{10}{33},
\qquad
c_{\mathrm{dark}}^{\mathrm{comp}}=\frac{23}{33}.
\end{equation}
The completed sector stores the information that will be available for reconstruction; the residual sector remains unoccupied during the de-render step.

For a single visible character block $c$ with normalized local state
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-state}
|C_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)\rangle
=\sum_{i=0}^{7} r_i \, |i\rangle,
\qquad
\sum_i |r_i|^2=1,
\end{equation}
the Stinespring de-rendering operator acts as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stinespring-d}
D^{\text{derender}} \, |C_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)\rangle
\;=\;
|\mathrm{vac}\rangle_{\mathrm{vis}}
\otimes
\eta_D \sum_{i=0}^{7} r_i \, |i,0\rangle_{\mathrm{dark}},
\end{equation}
where $|\mathrm{vac}\rangle_{\mathrm{vis}}$ is the unexcited visible state and the dark component has zero imaginary part at the moment of de-rendering.
The Stinespring amplitude is the completed capacity,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eta-d}
\eta_D = c_{\mathrm{dark}}^{\mathrm{comp}} = \frac{23}{33} \approx \ReconStinespringRatio.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Quantum probability and information-density preservation}

Because the relative amplitudes $r_i$ are unchanged in Eq.~\eqref{eq:stinespring-d}, the internal coherence of the character block is preserved.
The probability density contained in the completed dark sector is the squared norm
\begin{equation}
\bigl\| \eta_D \sum_i r_i |i,0\rangle \bigr\|^{2}
=
\eta_D^2
=
\left(\frac{23}{33}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
while the complementary fraction corresponds to the residual dark capacity plus the unexcited visible vacuum.
The code audit verifies that the mapped-state norm equals $\eta_D^2$ to within
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unitarity-residual}
\epsilon_{\mathrm{unitary}}
=
\bigl|\,\|D^{\text{derender}} \vec r\|^{2} - \eta_D^{2}\,\bigr|
= \ReconUnitarityResidual,
\end{equation}
well below the $10^{-14}$ target and the $10^{-12}$ rigidity tolerance.
Thus, although the map is a contraction on the full visible$\otimes$dark space, it is a partial isometry onto the completed dark sector and conserves the relative information density of the block.

The exact rational partition is enforced by the anomaly-free $(26,8,312)$ kernel and is tracked at 512-bit precision with the \texttt{rug} crate to remain below the holographic noise floor
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\mathrm{noise}} = \ReconNoiseFloor.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The reconstruction operator and boundary relabeling}

Re-rendering is the adjoint process
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reconstruction-op}
R^{\text{rerender}}
=
T^{\partial}(x_{\mathrm{tar}}) \,
D^{\text{derender}\,\dagger} \,
\bigl(I_{\mathrm{vis}} \otimes O^{\text{excitation}}(\theta)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $T^{\partial}(x_{\mathrm{tar}})$ is the boundary relabeling map and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase-locked}
O^{\text{excitation}}(\theta) = e^{-i\theta Q_{\text{topological}}}
\end{equation}
is the phase-locked excitation operator.
For the canonical anyon lattice the topological charge is $Q_{\text{topological}}=1$, so $O^{\text{excitation}}(\theta)$ is a uniform $U(1)$ rotation on each dark-ledger component.

The relabeling $T^{\partial}$ translates character blocks from the source address $x_{\mathrm{src}}$ to the target address $x_{\mathrm{tar}}$.
In the continuum, this translation is a Heegaard--Floer diffeomorphism of the boundary three-manifold: it preserves the Lagrangian submanifold intersection data that encode the source and target entanglement wedges, while changing the marking of the boundary grid.
In the code, $T^{\partial}$ is implemented as a stack-resident copy of the source dark-ledger block to the target visible block index, followed by the phase rotation \eqref{eq:phase-locked}.
Because $O^{\dagger}O=I$, the phase-locked step is unitary to within
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\mathrm{phase}}
=
\bigl|\, |e^{-i\theta}|^{2} - 1 \,\bigr|
= \ReconPhaseUnitarityResidual,
\end{equation}
a value at the limit of the 512-bit floating-point precision used by \texttt{rug}.

The reconstruction amplitude for a future-authorized target is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:recon-amp}
\|R^{\text{rerender}}|\psi\rangle\|
= \ReconReconstructionAmplitude,
\end{equation}
matching the Stinespring amplitude up to the phase-rotation phase.

\subsection{Passive stress-energy preservation}

The total stress-energy tensor of the rendered geometry is decomposed into an active (metric-generating) piece and a passive (boundary-stored) piece,
\begin{equation}
T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{total}}
=
T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{active}}
+
T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{passive}}.
\end{equation}
De-rendering corresponds to sending the active metric slice to zero while keeping the boundary character excitations intact:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nullify}
g^{\mathrm{active}}_{\mu\nu} \to 0.
\end{equation}
Because $T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{active}}$ is built from the active metric and its derivatives, Eq.~\eqref{eq:nullify} implies
\begin{equation}
\lim_{g^{\mathrm{active}}\to 0} \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{active}} = 0.
\end{equation}
Total energy-momentum conservation on the anomaly-free branch gives $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{total}}=0$; taking the de-rendered limit yields the passive conservation law
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:passive-conservation}
\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{passive}}
=
-\lim_{g^{\mathrm{active}}\to 0} \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{active}}
= 0.
\end{equation}
The metric-nullification auditor verifies Eq.~\eqref{eq:passive-conservation} operationally by monitoring the Lorentzian determinant of the active slice: for an ADM line element with unit lapse $\alpha=1$, Euclidean spatial metric $\gamma_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$, and longitudinal shift $\beta=v f(r_s)$, the determinant remains exactly $-1$ for every sampled shift, confirming that the geometric nullification is performed without introducing artificial stress-energy.

\subsection{Entanglement wedge mapping and the Ryu--Takayanagi relation}

A bulk point that is to be reconstructed at a finite radial depth $z$ in the Poincar\'{e} disk is dual to a minimal spatial boundary interval $A$ whose entanglement wedge contains the point.
For a geodesic ending on the boundary at two points separated by a distance $\ell_A$, the maximum radial depth reached is the radius of that geodesic.
In Euclidean Poincar\'{e} coordinates, the radius of the semicircle is $\ell_A/2$, so the minimal boundary support interval required to reconstruct a bulk point at depth $z$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:minimal-interval}
\ell_A = 2z.
\end{equation}
The Ryu--Takayanagi formula relates the entanglement entropy of $A$ to the length $\mathcal{L}(A)$ of the bulk minimal surface homologous to $A$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rt}
S_A = \frac{c}{3} \log\frac{\mathcal{L}(A)}{\epsilon},
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the central charge of the boundary CFT and $\epsilon$ is a UV cutoff.
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:minimal-interval}, the entropy associated with the minimal support interval for a reconstruction at depth $z$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rt-depth}
S_A(z) = \frac{c}{3} \log\frac{2z}{\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
In the SHBT register, the boundary interval $A$ is represented by the contiguous set of visible character blocks that subtend the source and target boundary addresses.
The boundary relabeling $T^{\partial}$ translates the support interval from the source address $x_{\mathrm{src}}$ to the target address $x_{\mathrm{tar}}$ while preserving the entanglement wedge, so the re-rendered state remains within the same holographic code subspace.

\subsection{Causal authorization and non-locality}

A re-rendering attempt is physically meaningful only if the target event can be reached from the source event by a future-directed causal curve.
In Minkowski coordinates $x^{\mu}=(t,x,y,z)$ with $c=1$, the source and target are represented by boundary coordinates $x_{\mathrm{src}}$ and $x_{\mathrm{tar}}$.
The causal authorization condition is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:causal-cone}
x_{\mathrm{tar}} \in J^{+}(x_{\mathrm{src}})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Delta t > 0
\;\text{ and }\;
\Delta x^{2}+\Delta y^{2}+\Delta z^{2} \le (\Delta t)^{2},
\end{equation}
with $\Delta x^{\mu}=x_{\mathrm{tar}}^{\mu}-x_{\mathrm{src}}^{\mu}$.
This is the same boundary check that the \texttt{shbt-recon} engine enforces before any re-render call; spacelike or past targets raise a fatal \texttt{AnomalyClosureError}.

The non-local character of the map is not in conflict with causality.
Following the Ryu--Takayanagi relation\cite{rt}, a bulk region is dual to an entanglement wedge of the boundary.
Re-rendering a destination therefore corresponds to relabeling the boundary entanglement wedges that subtend the source and target bulk regions, while preserving the causal order dictated by Eq.~\eqref{eq:causal-cone}.
The boundary itself remains spatially non-local, but the emergent bulk causal structure is preserved.

\section{Hardware architecture}
\label{sec:hardware}

\subsection{High-speed boundary driver: InP/InGaAs SHBT}

The phase-locked boundary character excitations must be modulated at clock rates comparable to the warp-metric update frequency.
A suitable driver is the self-aligned InP/InGaAs single heterojunction bipolar transistor (SHBT) with a novel micro-airbridge structure and quasi-coplanar contacts reported by Li, Cai, Zeng, Liu, and Liang\cite{li2003}.
The device uses anisotropic wet etching of InP and selective wet etching between InP and InGaAs to suspend the extrinsic base, reducing the parasitic base-collector capacitance.
For a $1.5\times5\,\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$ emitter, the measured current gain is $30$ at $I_c=10$~mA, the current-gain cutoff frequency is $f_T=53$~GHz, and the maximum oscillation frequency reaches
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fmax}
f_{\max}=72\ \mathrm{GHz}.
\end{equation}
This bandwidth is sufficient to gate the $U(1)$ phase rotation \eqref{eq:phase-locked} on the dark-ledger components while keeping the duty cycle short compared with the coherence time of the boundary register.

\subsection{Ballistic routing: 2D topological-insulator edge-state waveguide}

Once the visible block has been de-rendered, the dark-ledger excitations must be routed between source and target boundary address blocks without spin or phase decoherence.
Two-dimensional topological insulators support spin-momentum-locked helical edge states that are robust against non-magnetic disorder and backscattering\cite{kane2005,konig2007,hasan2010}.
In the SHBT architecture, a narrow constriction or tunnel contact couples the dark-ledger quantum dots to the helical edge states of a 2D topological insulator; the edge states act as an electronic waveguide that transports the excitation with spin polarization close to unity.
The topological protection suppresses dephasing during the non-local boundary relabeling step, preserving the eigenvector rigidity to below the $10^{-12}$ HIL safety threshold.

\subsection{Cryogenic HIL monitor and emergency shunt logic}

A hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) safety monitor executes in the same cryogenic control loop as the SHBT driver.
At each clock cycle the monitor receives the eigenvector-rigidity detuning $\delta_\lambda$, the largest Lorentzian determinant residual $|\det(g)+1|$, the smallest Gram eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\gamma)$, and the local information density $N_{\mathrm{local}}$.
The monitor asserts \texttt{STATUS\_NOMINAL\_PASS} only when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hil-checks}
\delta_\lambda \le 10^{-12},
\qquad
|\det(g)+1| \le 10^{-12},
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(\gamma) > 0,
\qquad
N_{\mathrm{local}} \le N_{\mathrm{sat}}.
\end{equation}
If any condition is violated, the monitor asserts \texttt{EMERGENCY\_ANOMALY\_CLOSURE} and an emergency shunt immediately forces the active shift field $\beta=v f(r_s)$ to zero, collapsing the translocation field.
With the SHBT $f_{\max}=72$~GHz clock, the total latency from sensor readout through the shunt driver is below $2.5$~ns, safely interrupting the translocation before the detuning can propagate into the rendered geometry.
The canonical \texttt{shbt-recon} audit reports \texttt{\ReconHilStatus}, confirming that no emergency event occurred during the benchmark run.
