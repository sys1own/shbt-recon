\title{Non-Local Boundary Address Relabeling and the Stinespring Re-rendering Channel in Static Holographic Boundary Theory}
\author{SHBT Collaboration}
\noaffiliation
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We formulate the artificial de-rendering and re-rendering of bulk destination states as an isometric Stinespring dilation on the Static Holographic Boundary Theory (SHBT) boundary.
Working on the canonical anomaly-free branch $(26,8,312)$ established by the \texttt{shbt-precision} kernel, we derive the Stinespring operator $D^{\text{derender}}$, the exact dark-capacity partition into residual $(10/33)$ and completed $(23/33)$ fractions, and the reconstruction operator $R^{\text{rerender}}$.
We prove that re-rendering is authorized only when the target event lies inside the future causal cone of the source event, $x_{\mathrm{tar}}\in J^{+}(x_{\mathrm{src}})$, and that the phase-locked excitation $O^{\text{excitation}}(\theta)$ is unitary to within the holographic noise floor $10^{-122}$.
A reference Rust/Python implementation, \texttt{shbt-recon}, executes the full pipeline at 512-bit precision and reports a Stinespring ratio of $\ReconStinespringRatio$, a unitarity residual of $\ReconUnitarityResidual$, and an eigenvector-rigidity detuning below $\ReconDetuningTolerance$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and SHBT context}
\label{sec:intro}

Static Holographic Boundary Theory (SHBT) posits that spacetime geometry and matter are rendered images of boundary information, encoded in a finite-dimensional register of character excitations.
The present work extends the engineering benchmarks reported for the $(26,8,312)$ branch in \texttt{shbt-precision} and the Alcubierre-type longitudinal shift metrics of \texttt{shbt-warp}, where a $10\,\mathrm{m}$ warp bubble required an operational power of $142.08\,\mathrm{MW}$ while satisfying the weak and null energy conditions on the interior plateau\cite{shbtwarp}.

In SHBT, a bulk event is not a primitive object; it is the emergent image of a pattern of boundary character excitations.
The ``closure chain'' that protects the theory from unphysical geometries is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:closure-chain}
\text{modular invariance}
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\Delta_{\mathrm{fr}}=0
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
E_{\mu\nu}=0,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{\mathrm{fr}}$ is the scalar framing defect of the boundary register and $E_{\mu\nu}$ is the holographic stress-energy residual.
When the chain holds, the boundary is anomaly-free and the rendered geometry is geometrically and energetically consistent.

This paper concerns the next logical operation: not merely rendering a bulk slice, but\emph{de-rendering} it---i.e.~nullifying the active metric degrees of freedom while preserving the passive stress-energy---and subsequently\emph{re-rendering} a destination state at a new boundary address.
We model this as a Stinespring dilation on the boundary Hilbert space, followed by a phase-locked reconstruction operator.

\section{First-principles derivations}
\label{sec:derivations}

\subsection{The Stinespring dilation as artificial de-rendering}

Consider a visible boundary register $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{vis}}$ of dimension $K$ and a dark ledger space $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{dark}}$ that absorbs the information removed from the active metric.
The de-rendering operator is an isometry
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stinespring-d}
D^{\text{derender}} : \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{vis}} \longrightarrow \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{vis}} \otimes \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{dark}},
\end{equation}
satisfying $D^{\dagger} D = I_{\mathrm{vis}}$.
For each local character $c$ the explicit Kraus/Stinespring form is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kraus-form}
D^{\text{derender}} = \sum_{c} \; |\mathrm{vac}\rangle_{\mathrm{vis}} \langle C_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)| \otimes |\chi^{\text{res}}_{c},0\rangle_{\mathrm{dark}},
\end{equation}
where $|C_{\mathrm{loc}}(c)\rangle$ is the visible character block, $|\mathrm{vac}\rangle_{\mathrm{vis}}$ is the unexcited visible state, and $|\chi^{\text{res}}_{c},0\rangle$ is the dark residual with zero imaginary component.
The operator removes the local visible excitation and deposits it, scaled by the Stinespring amplitude $\eta_{D}$, into the dark ledger.

The dark ledger capacity is split into two complementary fractions:
\begin{align}
c^{\text{res}}_{\text{dark}} &= \frac{10}{33}, \\
c^{\text{comp}}_{\text{dark}} &= \frac{23}{33}.
\end{align}
The Stinespring amplitude is the completed fraction,
\begin{equation}
\eta_{D} = c^{\text{comp}}_{\text{dark}} = \frac{23}{33} \approx \ReconStinespringRatio.
\end{equation}
This exact rational partition is enforced by the anomaly-free $(26,8,312)$ kernel and is tracked at 512-bit precision to remain below the holographic noise floor
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\text{noise}} = \ReconNoiseFloor.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Algebraic partitioning and information transfer limits}

The isometry condition $D^{\dagger}D=I$ implies that the total probability is conserved while the active metric information is redistributed.
For a single visible block of residual state $\vec{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{8}$ with $\|\vec{r}\|=1$, the dark-ledger state after mapping is $\eta_{D}\,\vec{r}$.
Its squared norm is therefore $\eta_{D}^{2}$, which is the fraction of information retained by the dark sector for later reconstruction.
The residual fraction $c^{\text{res}}_{\text{dark}}$ governs the complementary ``unused'' capacity that must remain dark to avoid information overcounting.

The numerical implementation verifies isometry directly: for a canonical residual input it computes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unitarity-residual}
\epsilon_{\text{unitary}} = \bigl|\,\|D^{\text{derender}}\vec{r}\|^{2} - \eta_{D}^{2}\,\bigr| = \ReconUnitarityResidual,
\end{equation}
well below the $10^{-12}$ rigidity threshold.

\subsection{The reconstruction operator}

Re-rendering is the adjoint process
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reconstruction-op}
R^{\text{rerender}} = T^{\partial}(x_{\mathrm{tar}}) \, D^{\text{derender}\,\dagger} \, \bigl(I_{\mathrm{vis}} \otimes O^{\text{excitation}}(\theta)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $T^{\partial}(x_{\mathrm{tar}})$ is the boundary relabeling map that translates character blocks from the source address $x_{\mathrm{src}}$ to the target address $x_{\mathrm{tar}}$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase-locked}
O^{\text{excitation}}(\theta) = e^{-i\theta Q_{\text{topological}}}
\end{equation}
is the phase-locked excitation operator.
For the canonical anyon lattice the topological charge is $Q_{\text{topological}}=1$, so $O^{\text{excitation}}(\theta)$ is a uniform $U(1)$ rotation.
Because $O^{\dagger}O=I$, the phase-locked step is unitary to within
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\text{phase}} = \ReconPhaseUnitarityResidual,
\end{equation}
a value at the limit of the 512-bit floating-point precision used by the \texttt{rug} crate.

The reconstruction amplitude for a future-authorized target is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:recon-amp}
\|R^{\text{rerender}}\,|\psi\rangle\| = \ReconReconstructionAmplitude,
\end{equation}
matching the Stinespring ratio up to the phase-rotation phase.

\section{Causal authorization and non-locality}
\label{sec:causal}

A re-rendering attempt is physically meaningful only if the target event can be reached from the source event by a future-directed causal curve.
In Minkowski coordinates $x^{\mu}=(t,x,y,z)$ with $c=1$, the source and target are represented by boundary coordinates $x_{\mathrm{src}}$ and $x_{\mathrm{tar}}$.
The causal authorization condition is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:causal-cone}
x_{\mathrm{tar}} \in J^{+}(x_{\mathrm{src}})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Delta t > 0
\;\text{ and }\;
\Delta x^{2}+\Delta y^{2}+\Delta z^{2} \le (\Delta t)^{2},
\end{equation}
with $\Delta x^{\mu}=x_{\mathrm{tar}}^{\mu}-x_{\mathrm{src}}^{\mu}$.
This is the same boundary check that the \texttt{shbt-recon} engine enforces before any re-render call.

The non-local character of the map is not in conflict with causality.
Following the Ryu--Takayanagi relation \cite{rt}, a bulk region is dual to an entanglement wedge of the boundary.
Re-rendering a destination therefore corresponds to relabeling the boundary entanglement wedges that subtend the source and target bulk regions, while preserving the causal order dictated by equation \eqref{eq:causal-cone}.
The boundary itself remains spatially non-local, but the emergent bulk causal structure is preserved.

\section{Computational implementation and results}
\label{sec:implementation}

The reference implementation, \texttt{shbt-recon}, is written in Rust with PyO3 bindings and exposes a Python package.
It uses the \texttt{rug} crate to perform 512-bit rational and floating-point arithmetic, keeping all register operations above the holographic noise floor.
The visible state vector is a stack-allocated array of 16 visible blocks, each carrying an 8-component dark ledger, avoiding heap allocations and preserving the integrity of high-frequency braiding loops.
